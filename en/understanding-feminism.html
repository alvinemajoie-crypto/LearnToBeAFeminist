Understanding feminism

Simple landmarks to discover the subject, without jargon.

What is feminism?

Feminism is a set of ideas and movements that defend equal rights and opportunities for women and men, and that challenge discrimination based on gender.

There is not one feminism but several currents, shaped by the history and realities of each society. What links them is the conviction that inequalities between the sexes are not natural, and that they can be changed.

A short glossary

Gender
The roles, behaviours and expectations a society associates with women and men. It differs from biological sex.
Equality and equity
Equality means the same rights for everyone. Equity means taking different starting points into account to reach real equality.
Patriarchy
A social system in which power and resources are mostly held by men.
Sexism
An attitude, remark or behaviour that disadvantages a person because of their sex or gender.
Stereotype
A ready-made idea about a group of people (“girls are gentle”, “boys don’t cry”) that limits everyone’s choices.
Intersectionality
The idea that several forms of discrimination (gender, origin, social class, disability…) can combine. Read the article.
Gender-based violence
Violence committed against a person because of their gender: physical, sexual, psychological and economic violence.
Empowerment
Giving people, and women in particular, more power over the decisions that affect their lives.
